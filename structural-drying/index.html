No part of restoring a water-damaged building matters more than structural drying, yet it is also where untrained crews make the most mistakes. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. A structure left wet ends up with odors, wood rot, mold and failing materials, so the moisture has to be removed in a scientific way using dehumidification and controlled evaporation.

Rochester Restoration Co employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. That knowledge lets them work out where equipment goes, what temperature to hold and how air should move so evaporation is as effective as possible on every job. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Daily monitoring is built into how we dry a structure, and it relies on calibrated meters, thermal cameras and thermo-hygrometers. We document moisture readings throughout the drying process, creating a detailed drying log that shows progressive moisture reduction in all affected materials. Those readings guide daily equipment adjustments that keep the drying as efficient as it can be. Drying is complete only when all affected materials reach acceptable moisture standards as defined by IICRC S500 guidelines, not when surfaces simply feel dry to the touch.

Signs You Need Structural Drying in Rochester

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Wood flooring with boards that cup, crown or pull apart
  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet
  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Condensation forming on windows in the area that was wet
  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Earlier water damage that was never professionally dried and now shows deterioration
  • Ceilings, floors or walls that still read high on a moisture meter following a leak

How to Prevent Structural Drying Problems

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Air movers need a clear path, so avoid stacking things on the equipment or in front of it
  • Address any water intrusion immediately, because the faster drying begins, the better the outcome
  • Plan for daily access so readings can be taken and equipment adjusted
  • The dehumidifiers and air movers should run around the clock until our technician takes them out
  • Each unit is positioned according to the moisture map, so ask before shifting anything
  • Trust the moisture meter readings, because surfaces can feel dry while moisture remains deep in materials

Why Hire a Professional for Structural Drying?

Structural drying is a science, not a guessing game. Secondary mold damage most often traces back to someone who put down fans and hoped things would dry. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Lacking that understanding, people either stop too soon and leave moisture for mold, or dry too hard and harm wood and other materials.

Consumer products and the equipment professionals dry structures with have very little in common. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.
