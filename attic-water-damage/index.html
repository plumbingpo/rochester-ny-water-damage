Attic Water Damage: What Rochester Homeowners Should Know

Water reaches an attic from two directions. Worn out shingles, flashing that has failed, and the seals around chimneys and vents all let rain in from overhead. Moisture also rises from the house below and condenses on cold roof sheathing when the attic is not ventilated well. In both cases, almost nobody climbs up to check. Our team in Rochester inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

Wet insulation is the heart of most attic jobs. Once wet, both cellulose and fiberglass lose R-value, and heating and cooling the home costs more. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. Our process is to pull the saturated insulation, dry the wood using dehumidifiers and air movers, and treat mold found on rafters or sheathing.

Warning Signs

  • Black or white fuzzy growth on the sheathing, often heaviest near the eaves
  • Nail points coming through the sheathing that are rusty or beaded with water
  • Brown rings or stains on an upstairs ceiling, often worse after rain
  • Bubbling or peeling ceiling paint in a room on the top floor
  • Daylight showing around a vent pipe, chimney, or along a roof valley
  • Dripping, rusted or water marked ducts and metal hardware up in the attic
What We Do

Our Attic Water Damage Process

1

Inspect and Trace

Our inspection covers the flashing points, roof deck, exhaust ducts and vents, and tells us if the water is condensation, a leak, or both. Its full reach is mapped with a thermal camera and meter readings.

2

Take Out Wet Insulation

Saturated or moldy insulation is bagged and removed so the wood and drywall under it can dry. Rooms under the attic are protected, and the route in and out stays contained.

Rafters, sheathing and joists are dried by air movers and dehumidifiers until their moisture levels are normal. Visible mold is cleaned from the wood and an antimicrobial is applied.

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. When the attic has stayed dry, the R-value is restored with new insulation.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

The first storm

Insulation Takes It

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. Nothing shows indoors, so the leak goes unnoticed and the roof is not repaired.

24 to 48 hours

Mold May Begin

Mold does very well on wet paper facing and damp lumber when the attic is warm and the air is still. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

A few weeks on

Stains Come Through

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

After months

Rot Sets In

Roofing nails loosen as the sheathing delaminates. Rafters stay damp enough to decay, and mold covers large areas of the roof deck.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Mold on Sheathing

With poor ventilation, mold commonly grows on the bottom of the roof deck. It can cover a wide area before anyone goes up to look.

Misrouted Exhaust Fans

Some bathroom fans exhaust into the attic and never reach the outdoors, adding warm, damp air daily. On cold sheathing that air condenses, and the result is easy to mistake for a leaking roof.

Insulation That Stops Working

Once damp and packed down, insulation does little of its job and might not recover even when dry. Rooms under the wet section run hotter or colder, and energy bills rise.

A Falling Ceiling

Saturated insulation is heavy. The ceiling drywall under it weakens where it is fastened, and it can give way suddenly and bring the wet insulation along.
