Water Heater Leak Cleanup Help in Rochester

The tank on a water heater stores 40 to 80 gallons, yet a leak can release far more than that. The tank is connected to your supply line, so when the bottom rusts through it refills as fast as it empties. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. The location of the heater sets the damage. It may be a hallway closet, a garage floor or, the worst case, an attic over finished rooms.

In Rochester, we start with water extraction and then inspect everything around the heater. The platform beneath the tank and the base of the closet walls are usually soaked. In a garage, water reaches the wall shared with the house and anything stored on the floor. Water from an attic unit soaks through insulation, then through the ceiling. We measure each area and dry it with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Right Now

Your First Steps

  1. Cut the Power or GasIf the heater is electric, turn its breaker off. On a gas unit, rotate the gas control to its off setting. Running the burner or the element with no water in the tank is something to avoid every time.
  2. Shut the Cold Water ValveThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. When that valve leaks or will not move, close the main water valve to the house.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Pets and children should stay clear, and nobody should walk into pooled water before it cools down.
  4. Empty the Tank OutsideWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorAnything absorbent, including boxes and rugs, should come up from the wet area. Fabric and cardboard absorb water fast, then keep it pressed to the concrete and walls.
Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Remove Standing Water

Standing water is taken up from the garage, closet or floor. With an attic unit we also clear the ceiling cavity and overflow pan, which keeps nearby materials from soaking up more.

2

To learn the full reach of the water, our crew meters and scans the platform, the bottoms of the walls, neighboring rooms and the ceiling below if there is one.

Soaked insulation, crumbling drywall and damaged baseboards are taken out. Attic jobs include removing wet insulation to expose the joists and the ceiling drywall for drying.

Air movers and dehumidifiers run for the 3 to 5 days drying usually takes. Moisture readings are recorded, and you hear from us once the space can take the new heater.

The Usual Culprits

What Causes It

  • Years of sediment buildup and corrosion wearing a hole in the base of an old steel tank.
  • Corroded inlet and outlet fittings or flex connectors on top of the heater.
  • Water discharged when the pressure and temperature relief valve fails or opens.
  • Drain pans beneath closet or attic units that rust through or clog and then spill over.
  • Anode rods that are used up, leaving the steel inside the tank open to rust.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

From a heater closet, the leak slips beneath the door and reaches hallway hardwood, laminate or carpet. Hot water speeds up swelling in wood flooring and loosens carpet backing.

Closet Walls

Water wicks up the drywall around an interior heater closet and into the rooms on the other side. Baseboards swell and the lower wall stays wet inside.

Stored Belongings

The first casualties are usually the furniture, boxes and keepsakes stored around the heater in a garage or closet. Cardboard falls apart, and wet contents can mold if they are not dried or discarded quickly.

Platform and Subfloor

The wooden stand or subfloor under the tank takes the most water. It can rot and weaken, which matters when a new full tank is set on it.

Coverage

What About Insurance?

Water damage to belongings, walls and floors from a tank that fails suddenly is often covered as a sudden and accidental loss. Usually the unit is left out of the claim, as age related wear is viewed as a maintenance matter. Rust trails, old staining or other evidence that the leak was long-term can lead an adjuster to reduce or deny the claim, since gradual leaks usually fall outside coverage. Read your policy, take photos of the damage and the heater, and do not dispose of the old unit before the claim is settled.

Pricing

What Drives the Price

  • Amount of contents affected, since wet belongings have to be relocated, dried or discarded.
  • Any rebuilding of the subfloor or platform beneath the tank ahead of the new heater.
  • How much insulation, ceiling or wall drywall needs to be taken out.
  • The location of the heater, since a garage leak costs less than one in an interior closet or attic.
