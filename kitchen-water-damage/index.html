Kitchen Water Damage in Rochester

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Nearly all of them are hidden inside a cabinet or behind an appliance. When one drips, the water runs to the back of the cabinet base and spreads under the floor. Our Rochester crew locates the leak, dries what it wet, and keeps as much of your kitchen intact as we can.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. By removing toe kicks and drilling small holes where they will not show, we push dry air beneath and behind the cabinets, and in many kitchens the countertops and cabinets never move.

Warning Signs

  • Sounds of dripping or hissing with all taps closed, or an unexplained rise in the water bill
  • Behind the dishwasher or sink, a wall with staining or blistered paint
  • Swollen or darkened toe kicks, or ones separating from the base of the cabinets
  • Downstairs, a brownish mark on the ceiling beneath the kitchen
  • Wood or laminate flooring that cups or lifts in front of the dishwasher
  • Cabinet doors that stick or no longer line up with each other
Our Process

Our Kitchen Water Damage Process

1

Shut Off the Leak

We shut off the leaking fixture or appliance line, pull the dishwasher or refrigerator if needed, and confirm exactly which connection failed so it can be repaired before drying begins.

2

Map the Moisture

The base of each cabinet, the wall in back and flooring all the way across the kitchen are checked with meters and a thermal camera. Under kitchen flooring, water often goes farther than anyone expects.

3

Dry in Place

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. An antimicrobial is applied to damp cavities while air movers and dehumidifiers work over several days.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. Once readings confirm everything is dry, we put the appliances back and help you schedule whatever repairs are left.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

The Ice Maker Line

The thin tube feeding the ice maker can leak a few drops a minute. Since the refrigerator is never pulled out, even that much rots the floor and the wall in back of it.

Under the Base Cabinets

Every side of the space beneath base cabinets is closed off. Water collects there, stays for weeks, and grows mold that nobody sees until the cabinets are pulled.

Behind the Dishwasher

The subfloor and neighboring cabinet sides get wet when the drain or supply connection drips slowly. Until the floor changes, the dishwasher covers everything.

Wet Wall Behind Cabinets

Supply and drain lines run through the wall behind the sink and dishwasher. Cabinets fully cover that drywall and framing, so a leak can soak them and mold can spread out of sight.

The Clock

The Damage Timeline

The first hour

Water Goes Underneath

Water from a failed hose or fitting flows to the back of the cabinets and under the appliances. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Days 3 through 7

Floors Begin Moving

As adhesive releases, vinyl begins lifting, while hardwood boards cup and laminate edges swell. The doors and drawers on the cabinets go out of alignment.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

A cabinet box that has swollen is not coming back. Subfloor may be delaminating, and mold behind the cabinets often means removing them along with the countertop above.
