Wet Carpet Drying: What Rochester Homeowners Should Know

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. The face fibers can feel only damp while the pad underneath is soaked like a sponge and the subfloor below it is wet too. What you can pull out with towels or a shop vac comes from the top, not from below. Our team in Rochester extracts the water, checks every layer with moisture meters, and sets up drying that reaches all the way down.

Saving a carpet comes down to how long it stayed wet and whether the water that soaked it was clean. When a clean supply line is the source and we arrive early, drying the carpet where it lies usually works. Water from an appliance counts as gray, so the pad is frequently removed while the carpet is cleaned and treated. Sewage or outside flood water means carpet and pad both come out.

Signs to Look For

  • Lift a corner and the backing peels away from the carpet
  • Wet tack strip showing through as dark, rusty streaks next to the baseboard
  • Baseboards that have swollen or paint that bubbles low on the wall
  • Ripples or loose spots where the carpet has let go of the tack strip
  • A wet spot that keeps growing even though no new water has been added
  • Soft or squeaky subfloor beneath the area of carpet that was soaked

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Moves Outward

Water travels through the pad far beyond the patch you can see. It slips beneath walls into neighboring rooms while the tack strip begins to absorb it.

Hours 24 to 48

Smell and Mold Start

That sour wet carpet odor shows up as bacteria multiply in the damp pad. Dust held down in the fibers and the carpet backing both give mold a place to begin.

Day 3 to 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

Ripples appear and the carpet delaminates because the latex in its backing is breaking down. Rust forms on the tack strip nails while the subfloor underneath swells.

After a week

Too Late to Save

Carpet and pad left wet this long generally cannot stay. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.

Hidden Risks

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Mold on the Backing

Mold tends to start underneath, on the carpet back, because that side stays damp and dark. Your nose finds it well before anything shows on the surface.

Wet Wall Bottoms

The base of each wall is where water moving beneath the carpet ends up. It wicks up into the baseboards and drywall unnoticed, since the carpet hides that edge.

Subfloor Damage

Particleboard and OSB under carpet swell and crumble when they stay wet. The soft spots, squeaking and lifted seams appear weeks afterward, when nobody remembers the spill.

Furniture Marks

Furniture legs sitting on wet carpet release wood stain and rust into the fibers. Those marks are often permanent, and the furniture itself wicks water and swells.

Our Process

Our Wet Carpet Drying Process

1

Map the Wet Area

Our first job is finding the outer limit of the water, under walls and in closets too, using a thermal camera and moisture meters. Then we determine which category of water it is, as that tells us what is safe to keep.

2

Pull Out the Water

Weighted extraction tools press water out of the carpet and the pad together. Drying goes faster afterward, because far more water comes out this way than with only a wand.

3

Dry Every Layer

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Air movers and dehumidifiers run until carpet and subfloor both read dry.

4

Clean and Reset

Last, we apply antimicrobial to carpet and subfloor if they need it, replace any pad that was taken out, stretch the carpet back over the tack strip, and give it a cleaning.
