How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Rochester?

Home / Blog / How Much Does Water Damage Cost in Rochester?

Typical Cost at a Glance

Most water damage jobs in Rochester fall somewhere between $1,500 and $10,000 or more. A minor job involving clean water may cost less than roughly $1,500. At the other end, heavy flooding or sewage with reconstruction work can go well beyond $10,000.

These are typical national ranges, not a quote. Local labor rates and material prices in Rochester, NY move the numbers up or down. An exact price requires someone to inspect the home in person and take moisture readings.

Cost by Water Category

Restoration pricing usually starts with the type of water involved. The industry sorts water into three categories based on how contaminated it is. A job with dirty water calls for extra protective gear, more disinfecting and more material taken out.

Depending on which category applies, US cleanup costs are usually in the area of $3 to $7.50 a square foot. Here are the usual numbers.

CategoryWhat it isTypical cost per sq ft
Category 1, clean waterWater heater tanks, faucets and supply linesAbout $3 to $4
Category 2 (gray water)Failed sump pumps, discharge from a dishwasher or washing machineAbout $4 to $6.50
Category 3 (black water)Storm surge, flooding rivers, sewageAbout $7 to $7.50

A loss does not always stay in one category. Clean water that sits for a couple of days picks up bacteria and is then treated as gray or black water. That is one reason waiting costs money.

The Main Price Factors

The same kind of pipe failure may cost one homeowner much more than another. These are the items a contractor weighs in an estimate.

  • Area affected. A larger wet area takes extra crew hours, additional machines and a longer stay.
  • Water category. Jobs with clean water cost the least. Nothing costs more to deal with than sewage.
  • How long it sat. Given time, water works its way into wall cavities and subfloors. A leak that runs all weekend while you are gone costs far more than one caught in an hour.
  • Materials. Concrete and tile are easy to dry. Special drying or outright replacement is common for drywall, hardwood, insulation and carpet pad.
  • Mold. Existing growth means remediation work, including containment and filtered air, becomes part of the job.
  • Reconstruction. One part of the bill is drying. Replacing drywall, flooring, cabinets and paint afterward is a separate one, and on large jobs it is often the bigger of the two.

An estimate normally has two main parts, with water extraction coming before structural drying. Plan on 3 to 5 days for the drying in most cases.

Air movers and dehumidifiers are often billed by the day. A home that dries in three days costs less than one that takes six.

Cost by Common Job

These are typical national ranges for the jobs we see most often in Rochester. Treat them as a starting point, not a price.

  • Burst pipe. Cleaning up and drying out often runs $1,500 to $5,000, with repair of the burst pipe billed in addition. The total climbs when water flowed for hours or reached a second floor.
  • Appliance leak. Found early, a leak from a dishwasher, water heater or washing machine is often $1,000 to $4,000. Slow leaks under cabinets cost more because of hidden damage.
  • Basement flood. Depth, where the water came from and whether the room is finished put most jobs at $2,000 to $10,000 or more. Basement water removal in a finished basement sits at the high end.
  • Sewage backup. Commonly $3,000 to $10,000 and up. Anything porous has to go and all surfaces get disinfected, since cleaning up sewage counts as Category 3.
  • Roof leak. Often $1,000 to $5,000 for drying and repairing ceilings, insulation and walls, not counting the roof fix.

Is Water Damage in Rochester Covered by Insurance?

The answer turns on your policy and on what caused the damage. Sudden, accidental damage, like a supply line failing without warning or a burst pipe, is often covered by a standard homeowners policy.

Gradual leaks are usually not covered. An insurer may treat a fitting that dripped for months as a maintenance problem. Flood water from outside, such as a rising river or storm surge, is usually excluded unless you carry separate flood insurance. A backed-up sewer often takes an added endorsement.

Before assuming anything, read the policy or phone your agent. Remember to consider your deductible. A small repair may come in under the deductible, in which case filing may not be worthwhile.

Ways to Lower the Cost

How much you pay for water damage is largely settled within a day or two. A few simple steps make a real difference.

  • Act fast. Water left in place goes on spreading. More material gets wet with each passing hour.
  • Shut off the water. The moment you discover a leak, close the main shutoff, which means knowing in advance where to find it.
  • Document everything. Before moving or discarding anything, record it in photos and on video. Hold on to all receipts.
  • Call a pro before mold starts. Mold can begin growing in 24 to 48 hours. After that point, expect added time and added cost.
  • Move what you can. Get rugs, furniture and boxes off wet floors if it is safe.

Do not walk through standing water if the power is on, and stay out of anything that may contain sewage. No saving is worth a shock or an infection. Water that entered from outdoors calls for professional flood cleanup by people wearing the correct protection.

Getting a Real Number

A national range helps with budgeting, yet no house is the average house. Only a moisture meter inspection reveals where the water went and what drying and repairs will be needed.

Damage only gets bigger with time, so call for restoration of water damage in Rochester, NY and have the inspection done now.
