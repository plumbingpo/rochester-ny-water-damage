Roof Leak Water Damage in Rochester: What to Know

Where you see the stain and where the rain gets in are usually two different places. Water enters at a raised shingle, a split in the flashing or a worn skylight seal, follows the rafters and decking downhill, and finally lands on the insulation. By the time a brown ring shows on the ceiling, the attic above it has often been wet for a while. We find the full wet area and dry it out.

We are a water damage crew, not a roofing company. Our job is the inside of the house: pulling out soaked insulation, drying the framing and drywall, treating for mold and putting the ceiling back together. When the roof is still letting water through, a temporary tarp is something we can install, and we will coordinate with your roofer so the repair comes before the ceiling is closed.

Do This First

What to Do Right Now

  1. Shut Off Wet CircuitsIf water is dripping through a light fixture, a ceiling fan or near an outlet, switch off that circuit at the breaker and leave the fixture alone.
  2. Catch the DripsSet buckets or pans under active drips and move furniture, rugs and electronics out of the way. Plastic or towels spread on the floor will keep it from soaking.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeIf the ceiling is holding a pocket of water, stand to one side and poke a small hole at the lowest point so it drains into a bucket.
  4. Stay Off the RoofClimbing on slick shingles or crawling around a dark attic is not worth the risk. Note from inside where the water is coming in and leave the rest to professionals.
  5. Photograph the DamageBefore anything is moved or discarded, get video and photos of the attic, the ceiling and damaged items, and then phone us so drying can begin.
Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Roof Leak Water Damage

Homeowners policies often cover interior water damage when a roof leak is sudden and accidental, such as wind tearing off shingles or a limb puncturing the roof. Insurers generally turn down claims tied to wear, age or lack of upkeep, and they may handle the roof repair on different terms than the inside damage. Our team gives your adjuster photos and moisture readings, yet what is covered comes down to your policy, so check it or talk to your agent.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Nails that have backed out of the decking and left small holes through the shingles.
  • Storm impact, such as hail or a tree branch, breaking through the shingles.
  • Wind-torn, curling or split shingles that leave the underlayment open to rain.
  • Skylight seals and curbs that dry out, crack and let rain run inside.
  • Sun-cracked rubber collars where plumbing vent pipes come through the roof.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Wall Cavities

Water that reaches the top of a wall runs down inside it, wetting insulation and the back of the drywall. You might notice nothing more than a peeling patch or a puffy baseboard.

Ceiling Fixtures and Wiring

Recessed cans, ceiling fans and electrical boxes are low points where roof water collects and drips through. Keep that circuit off until someone has inspected it, since damp wiring may corrode or short out.

Ceiling Drywall

Staining, sagging and loss of strength follow when drywall is not dried. The tape releases along cracked seams, and with enough weight a section can come down suddenly.

Attic Insulation

For days after a leak, matted insulation sits wet on top of the ceiling and no longer insulates. Fiberglass can sometimes be dried. Removal is the usual answer for wet cellulose.
