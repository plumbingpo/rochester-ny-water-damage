Put simply, mitigation is the work of holding damage to its current size. Do not confuse it with rebuilding. It is everything done before the repair to protect what is still sound. For Rochester homes, that means we stop the source, pull the water out, seal off wet areas and get the structure stable. Good mitigation makes the later repair smaller, and now and then makes it unnecessary.

The words mitigation and restoration get used as if they were interchangeable. Each is its own phase of the job. Mitigation is the emergency phase: stop the loss, limit the spread, stabilize the home. Restoration is what comes after: drying is confirmed, and damaged drywall, flooring and finishes are repaired or rebuilt. Insurers treat them as separate line items too, so it helps to know which phase you are in.

A typical mitigation visit covers four things. Source control, so no new water enters. Two: extract whatever water is standing. Three: contain the area with plastic sheeting and managed airflow, so clean rooms stay free of moisture and contaminants. Four: stabilize by pulling materials beyond saving, applying antimicrobial to surfaces and setting up air movers plus dehumidifiers. Along the way, your belongings and furniture get moved or set up on blocks to keep them safe.

Water Damage Mitigation: What Homeowners Ask

Clean water, for instance from a supply line, is Category 1. With Category 2, think dishwasher or washer overflow that contains a degree of contamination. Category 3 is heavily contaminated and includes sewage and flood water from outside. Clean water can move to a worse category the longer it sits, which changes how we treat it.
Not everything releases water, even with air movers and dehumidifiers running. Typical cases include soaked carpet padding, baseboards that have swelled and drywall that crumbles. If they stay, the subfloor and framing remain damp and the whole job dries more slowly. Removal decisions come from meter readings, and each removed item is listed for your insurance file.
New water stops once the plumber is done, while the water that got out stays where it went. Wet drywall, flooring and cabinets keep absorbing moisture until it is extracted and dried. Mold can begin within 24 to 48 hours in damp materials. If anything beyond a hard surface got wet, have the moisture checked.
The job of mitigation is to limit the damage. That includes controlling the source, extracting water, setting containment and stabilizing the house for drying. Next is restoration, which puts the home back as it was with repairs like new flooring, drywall and paint. Mitigation is urgent and measured in days. Scheduled restoration work may run longer based on the extent of damage.
Often the first visit is enough to control the source, get the water out and put containment up. After that, drying usually runs 3 to 5 days while we monitor moisture readings. Contaminated water, bigger losses and dense materials such as plaster or hardwood may extend that. We give you a realistic schedule after the first inspection.

How to Prevent Water Damage Mitigation Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Learn where your main shutoff is and confirm the valve still turns.
  • Keep gutters clear and downspouts pointed away from the foundation of the house.
  • Replace rubber washing machine hoses with braided steel lines every five years or so.
  • Once a month, look beneath sinks and near toilets for stains, drips or soft spots in the floor.
  • Roof penetrations, door frames and window edges should be sealed ahead of the next big storm.
  • Get the water heater checked every year, and replace it ahead of a tank failure.

What to Expect From Our Water Damage Mitigation

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle water damage mitigation from start to finish:

Control the Source

The supply gets shut off, the broken line capped, or the opening letting water in tarped and boarded. When the permanent repair calls for a roofer or plumber, we help schedule it.

2

Warning Signs for Water Damage Mitigation

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Bathroom or kitchen cabinets are sitting over water that cannot be reached to dry.
  • Water is no longer flowing, though it has left wet floors, cabinets or walls behind.
  • An adjuster or agent said you must reasonably limit additional damage.
  • The house feels muggy and window glass is wet with condensation since the leak.
  • One day on, damp materials are beginning to smell musty.
  • The carpet pad is soaked through even where the surface seems only damp.
  • An opening in the roof or at a window lets rainwater enter the building.

What a Professional Brings to Water Damage Mitigation

Fast decisions, one after another, are what mitigation consists of. Can this section of drywall dry in place, or does it need cutting out? Has the water shifted category since the leak started, and is it Category 1, 2 or 3 now? Does the cabinet toe kick need to come off? Our team makes these calls from training and from moisture readings, not from how a surface looks or feels to the hand.

Your insurance claim benefits from proper mitigation too. Owners are required by most policies to take reasonable steps against further damage, and the insurer will ask for evidence. On arrival we take photos, then log moisture readings every day and itemize whatever is removed. That file shows what the water did and what was done about it. Whoever handles repairs then begins from a known condition, and nothing wet ends up sealed behind new wallboard.
