Hardwood Floor Water Damage: What Rochester Homeowners Should Know

It does not take long for water to change a wood floor. Moisture enters each board through its underside and edges, the boards swell and press together, and cupping begins as the edges lift above the middle. That does not mean the floor is lost. When drying starts within the first few days, solid hardwood frequently lies flat again. What matters is removing moisture from the wood and from its subfloor steadily and under control.

We dry hardwood with floor drying mats. A vacuum unit attached to the sealed mats lifts water out by way of the seams and the wood itself, and dehumidifiers hold the room air at a dry level. Compared with carpet or drywall it is slower, commonly a week or more. No sanding should happen before the wood has dried completely. A cupped floor sanded early ends up crowned afterward.

Signs to Look For

  • Finish turning white, cloudy or peeling in the wet area
  • A springy or soft spot in the floor that was not there earlier
  • Board centers humped higher than the edges, called crowning
  • Creaking and popping noises from boards that used to be quiet
  • Dark gray or black stains along seams and around nail heads
  • Swollen trim along the edge of the floor near the leak
What You Cannot See

The Damage You Do Not See

Sanding Too Early

Leveling a damp, cupped floor with a sander takes wood off the lifted edges. When the wood finishes drying, the centers end up high and the floor is crowned.

Stains From Nails and Staples

Nails and staples that stay wet begin to rust and lose holding power. Dark marks from that rust soak into the boards and can be too deep to sand out.

Growth Beneath the Floor

The space between hardwood and subfloor is dark and has no airflow. You will smell it before you see it, because nothing shows on the surface.

Water That Keeps Moving

Moisture beneath flooring does not stay put. Cabinets and wall plates do not stop it, so it may enter adjacent rooms or come out at the ceiling one floor down.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Measure the Moisture

Readings are taken in the boards and the subfloor, and on dry flooring close by as a reference. From those numbers we learn the depth of the water and if the floor can realistically be saved.

2

Remove Surface Water

We extract any standing water immediately and clear furniture and rugs from the wet section. Cutting off the water entering the joints early keeps swelling down.

3

Install the Mat System

A vacuum system hooked to mats sealed on the surface lifts moisture out of the boards from above. The room is managed with dehumidifiers so drying stays even and the boards do not crack.

4

Track Progress and Report

Every day or two we take new readings, continuing until the subfloor and boards match the dry reference spots. At that point you hear from us if it needs nothing, some replacement boards, or a refinish.

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Seepage Below the Boards

Seams let the water through, and it fans out in the layer between flooring and subfloor. The surface may wipe dry while the underside stays soaked.

24 to 48 hours

Cupping Starts

Boards swell and the edges rise. A cloudy look can appear in the finish. The bare underside of the boards and the subfloor are where mold can start.

Day 3 to 7

Pressure Builds Up

Pressure grows through the whole floor as the wood expands. With fasteners losing their hold and boards compressing at the edges, some areas can lift away from the subfloor.

After a week

Permanent Damage

Buckling, black staining and mold under the boards become likely. By now replacement, not drying, is the answer for most of the affected section.
